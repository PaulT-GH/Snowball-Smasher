Debt snowball vs debt avalanche: which should you choose?

Two proven ways to pay off several debts. Here's how the snowball and avalanche methods differ, what each costs you, and how to pick one.

By Paul Thomas · 26 October 2026

If you owe money on several cards or loans, you need a rule for deciding where any spare cash goes. The two best-known rules are the debt snowball and the debt avalanche. They share the same basic idea and differ only in which debt gets the extra money first.

What they have in common

With both methods you pay the required payment on every debt, every month. You then pick one debt as your target and send all the remaining money to it. When the target is cleared, its payment rolls onto the next debt, so the amount going at your debt each month stays the same. That steady total is what gives the snowball its name.

The debt snowball: smallest balance first

You rank your debts from the smallest balance to the largest and attack the smallest first. You clear accounts quickly, which can feel motivating, and each cleared debt is one less payment to think about.

The catch: the smallest debt isn't always the most expensive one. You can pay more interest overall than you need to.

The debt avalanche: highest interest rate first

You rank your debts by interest rate (APR) and attack the highest first. Interest is what makes debt expensive, so this order usually costs you the least in total.

The catch: if your highest-rate debt is also a large one, it can be a long time before you clear anything. Some people find that hard to stick with.

How much difference does it make?

It depends on your debts. The gap is larger when balances and rates are far apart, and smaller when they are similar. In our worked example both methods cleared the debts in the same number of months, but the avalanche saved about £405 in interest.

How to choose

  • Pick the avalanche if you're driven by saving money and happy to wait for the first win.
  • Pick the snowball if you need early wins to keep going. A plan you stick to beats a better plan you abandon.
  • Not sure? Run both in the calculator and look at the difference in time and interest. If it's small, choose the one that feels better.
Whichever you choose, check whether any of your debts charge fees or penalties for early repayment, and always keep up the required payments on every debt.
