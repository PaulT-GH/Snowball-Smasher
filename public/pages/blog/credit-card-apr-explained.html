Credit card APR explained: how interest is really charged

What APR means, how it turns into monthly interest, and why it decides which debt to pay off first.

By Paul Thomas · 26 October 2026

APR stands for annual percentage rate. It's the yearly cost of borrowing, shown as a percentage. It matters because it tells you which of your debts is costing you the most.

Got your APR to hand? Enter it in the calculator and see your plan.

From APR to a monthly figure

As a rough guide, divide the APR by 12 to get a monthly rate. The Snowball Smasher calculator works this way:

CardAPRMonthly rateInterest on the first month
Card A, £8,00033.3%2.775%£222.00
Card B, £4,00018.9%1.575%£63.00

Card A is twice the size of Card B, but it costs more than three times as much in interest each month (£222.00 against £63.00).

Why real life is slightly different

Lenders may calculate interest daily on your balance and add it at the statement date. That can make the true cost a little higher or lower than the simple monthly division. For planning, the simple method is a good guide.

Where to find your APR

Look on your statement or in your card account. If you have more than one APR, such as one for purchases and a higher one for cash advances, use the rate that applies to your balance. If you don't know it, ask your lender.

Why APR matters for your plan

A higher APR means each pound of debt costs more every month. That is why the avalanche method, which pays the highest APR first, usually saves the most interest. Even if you use the snowball, knowing your rates shows you where your money is going.

Watch for promotional rates. A 0% deal that ends will switch to a higher standard rate, so enter the rate you'll pay after the offer, or run the calculator again when the rate changes.
