Why paying the minimum keeps you in debt for years

A minimum payment that shrinks as your balance falls can stretch a debt out for years. Here's why, and the simple fix.

By Paul Thomas · 26 October 2026

Credit card minimum payments are designed to be affordable. Unfortunately, that is also what keeps the debt alive for so long.

See what keeping your payment steady would do for you. Try it in the calculator.

Why the minimum shrinks

Most cards set the minimum as a percentage of what you owe, often plus the month's interest, with a small fixed amount as a floor. As you pay the balance down, the required payment falls too. Each month you owe a bit less and are asked to pay a bit less.

What that does to your timeline

In our worked example two cards total £12,000. Paying only the required 5% (minimum £100) each month, the monthly payment starts at £614.25 and drops to about £200 by the end. The debt takes 45 months to clear and costs £5,952.50 in interest.

Keeping the payment at £614.25 every month clears the same debt in 23 months, with £3,654.52 in interest (using the avalanche). Same starting point, but nearly half the time.

The simple fix: keep paying the same amount

Work out what you pay in total across your debts this month, and keep paying that amount when the required payments fall. The money you no longer have to pay goes straight onto your target debt. That is the whole idea behind the snowball.

How to do it in practice

  1. Add up this month's payments across every debt.
  2. Set up a standing order or direct debit for the target debt that is higher than its minimum.
  3. Review every few months as balances change, or whenever a debt is cleared.
