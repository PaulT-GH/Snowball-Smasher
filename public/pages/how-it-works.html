How It Works

Understanding the debt payoff methods and how to use our calculator to become debt-free faster.

Debt Snowball vs. Debt Avalanche

There are two primary strategies for paying off multiple debts: the Debt Snowball and the Debt Avalanche. Both methods have their advantages, and the best choice depends on your personal financial situation and motivation style.

Debt Snowball Method

How it works:

  1. List all your debts from smallest balance to largest
  2. Make minimum payments on all debts
  3. Put any extra money toward the smallest debt
  4. Once the smallest debt is paid off, add that payment amount to the next smallest debt
  5. Continue this process, creating a "snowball" effect as you pay off each debt

Advantages:

  • Quick wins provide psychological motivation
  • Simplifies your finances faster by eliminating accounts
  • Builds momentum and confidence

Best for:

People who need motivation from seeing progress and quick wins

Debt Avalanche Method

How it works:

  1. List all your debts from highest interest rate to lowest
  2. Make minimum payments on all debts
  3. Put any extra money toward the highest-interest debt
  4. Once the highest-interest debt is paid off, add that payment amount to the next highest-interest debt
  5. Continue this process until all debts are paid

Advantages:

  • Saves the most money in interest payments
  • Mathematically optimal approach
  • Typically results in faster overall debt payoff

Best for:

People who are motivated by saving money and prefer the most efficient approach

Tips for Successful Debt Payoff

1. Stick to Your Plan

Consistency is key. Make your debt payments a priority in your budget and stick to the plan even when it's tempting to spend elsewhere.

2. Find Extra Money

Look for ways to increase your income or reduce expenses to put more money toward debt. Even small amounts add up over time.

3. Avoid New Debt

While paying off existing debt, avoid taking on new debt. Consider using cash or debit cards instead of credit cards.

4. Celebrate Milestones

Celebrate when you pay off each debt, but do so in ways that don't involve spending a lot of money or creating new debt.

5. Update Your Plan

As your financial situation changes, update your debt payoff plan. Recalculate when you receive raises, bonuses, or unexpected windfalls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which method will save me the most money?

The Debt Avalanche method (paying highest interest rates first) will mathematically save you the most money in interest payments.

Which method will help me get out of debt faster?

The Debt Avalanche method typically results in faster overall debt payoff, but the difference is often small compared to the Snowball method.

What if I can't afford more than the minimum payments?

Even making just the minimum payments with a strategic approach (Snowball or Avalanche) is better than random payments. Look for ways to reduce expenses or increase income to find extra money for debt payoff.

Should I close credit card accounts after paying them off?

Generally, it's better to keep accounts open, especially older accounts, as they contribute positively to your credit history length. Just avoid using them to accumulate new debt.

What about debt consolidation or balance transfers?

These can be effective strategies if you qualify for lower interest rates. Our calculator can help you compare the savings from these approaches versus the Snowball or Avalanche methods.

How accurate are the calculator results?

Our calculator provides a good estimate based on the information you provide. However, actual results may vary due to factors like interest compounding methods, payment timing, and potential changes in interest rates.
